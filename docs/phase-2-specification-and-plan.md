## 1. Executive summary

**Settled product direction (including the 2026-10-14 authentication and 2026-10-15 guest-access amendments):** Cognito Google federation and email/password customer sign-in, a controlled admin catalog shared by all customer experiences, Discover with the established gestures, an OOTD curated vertical outfit feed, and a long-term hybrid retailer architecture. These are retained from the owner's review; they are not questions about whether to include them. Gate B is approved; neither this revision nor the product decisions authorize implementation.

Recommend **P2-A: controlled catalog administration, Cognito customer sign-in, authenticated wishlist and Discover**, followed by **P2-B: curated OOTD using the same catalog**. A customer sees one clothing product at a time, searches/filters, swipes left for similar, right to save, up for another brand, and taps for detail. A policy-approved merchant link may leave DRIP; DRIP does not transact in P2-A. No pilot brand or merchant participation is assumed.

Guest read-only browsing is approved for the initial release (D8), alongside Cognito Google/email-password sign-in. Guests can discover/swipe, search, filter, view eligible published product details and open approved retailer links without registering. A guest right-swipe preserves the current card and prompts sign-in; after authentication, require explicit confirmation and backend acknowledgement before reporting a save or advancing. No guest wishlist, silent merge or account-specific guest session is included. PostgreSQL-backed wishlist persistence and online cross-device refresh are approved in P2-A (D9). Authentication alone does not synchronize saves: dedicated persistence, authorized APIs and client refresh behavior are required. Local state is only an authenticated account-scoped cache, never authoritative saves.

Preserve the modular monolith, PostgreSQL authority, Redis ephemerality, exact money, UUIDv7 defaults, fail-closed configuration and React-free contracts. Retain seven catalog/publication tables with explicit necessity below; they are not the entire product schema. Identity/session/access and wishlist records are staged separately. Outfit records belong to P2-B; no inventory history, cart, payment or general ingestion-job tables in P2-A. The JSON CLI is an optional bootstrap/operations adapter to the same publication service used by the required admin UI.

The first product milestone is complete only when an authorized admin can manage real approved catalog data and a guest can browse eligible public products and approved retailer links without sign-in, then authenticate, confirm a save and see it across authenticated devices on tested mobile builds. Catalog and identity sub-milestones are independently verifiable dependencies. Without approved sources, show an honest empty state and leave launch acceptance incomplete. P2-A acceptance does not mean all Phase 2 work is complete: OOTD remains scheduled in P2-B with its own approval and acceptance evidence.

### Committed Phase 2 roadmap after the foundational milestone

- **P2-B curated OOTD vertical feed:** guest browsing is approved; implementation remains separately gated. Retained product scope, dependent on the shared catalog, controlled editorial administration, outfit/image rights and working customer navigation. Section 8 defines its data/API/UI acceptance. It is sequenced after P2-A, not removed or reduced to another product sort.

### Deferred platform capabilities

Spreadsheet imports/uploads, merchant self-service/onboarding, multi-operator collaborative draft workflows, automated retailer feeds, live inventory observations, automatic price refresh, personalized ranking, external search services, notifications, analytics SDKs and media processing. Admin catalog management itself is not deferred. Spreadsheet specification must cover ownership, preview, malicious inputs, row errors, retries and rollback before later authorization.

### Initial execution exclusions and hybrid extensibility

No cart/reservation, integrated checkout, payments/UPI, orders, settlement or marketplace seller execution in P2-A/P2-B. The long-term modes remain `external_redirect`, `integrated_retailer`, and `drip_marketplace`. Only external redirects may be enabled initially; service/DB constraints fail closed for other modes until a separately reviewed migration, capability/authorization design and integration tests enable them. This is a rollout restriction, not a permanent external-link-only architecture.

Brand, merchant, canonical product, listing, variant and price remain separate so future retailer adapters do not redefine product identity or wishlist IDs. Do not add speculative checkout/order tables or routes now. Any future DRIP-owned checkout must require authentication before order placement; this is a future constraint, not P2-A/P2-B implementation scope. A future seller may need listing-level capabilities rather than one merchant-wide mode; specify that when integrating it instead of inferring checkout support from a TS enum. No scraping without permission, LLM database access/price calculation, fabricated production data, inferred stock or discounts.

### Customer behavior and eligibility

- Published product + active brand + at least one published listing from an active merchant + an active variant with a current approved INR price is required for Discover. Visibility is checked at request time. Disabled/withdrawn records never reappear through a stale cursor.
- One card represents a canonical product, with a representative listing/variant chosen from the variants that satisfy **all** active filters. Do not match a cheap size on one variant and a requested color on another. Choose lowest eligible price, then listing UUID, then variant UUID; disclose "from" when eligible variant prices vary. No paid ranking.
- Search: normalized, case-insensitive literal substring of product title or brand display name; maximum 80 Unicode characters. Escape SQL LIKE wildcards; no fuzzy/semantic promises. PostgreSQL scan is acceptable for the bounded pilot; add search infrastructure only after measurement.
- Initial filters: brand IDs, category codes, normalized color family, merchant size labels, INR minimum/maximum. Size is a source label, not a cross-brand fit guarantee. Unknown size cannot satisfy a size filter. No availability filter while inventory is unknown.
- Left: candidate must be in the same category as the current card; rank matching color family first, then UUID. Compare the anchor and candidate representative variants selected under the bound filters at request time; NULL color earns no matching bonus. A price/content change may change that representative. This is the explicitly limited definition of "similar", not a learned recommendation or dislike record.
- Up: candidate must have a different canonical brand ID. Retain all user filters; a single-brand filter can legitimately yield no result. Explain the conflict and offer an explicit filter change; never relax filters silently.
- Right: idempotently save the product ID to the signed-in user's server wishlist, then request ordinary next discovery only after server acknowledgement. Failure/session expiry leaves the card in place with retry/sign-in; never announce success for a cache-only write. A guest must sign in and confirm the pending save under the transition contract below. Saving does not endorse or reserve stock.
- Tap opens detail without recording a preference. Back retains deck/filter state in memory. Gesture thresholds must avoid accidental saves while scrolling details; reduced-motion and screen-reader/button parity are acceptance requirements.
- No candidate: return an explicit reason (no eligible products, no similar candidate, no different brand, or session exhausted), with reset/change-filter actions. Do not cycle endlessly, invent alternatives, or substitute another swipe meaning.
- Session deduplication is bounded to 200 seen product IDs, not an enduring user profile. At the cap, ask to start a new session. Resetting filters starts a new session explicitly. Saved products may appear in later sessions with a saved indicator; a duplicate save never creates another server association or cache entry.

## 4. Proposed data model

All names and fields here are **proposed**, not files or migrations created by this task. Ownership: catalog controls brands/products/listings/variants/prices; the thin merchant registry controls seller identity/destinations; operator publication orchestrates these services in one PostgreSQL transaction. Discovery depends on catalog query DTOs, not writes to catalog tables. The wishlist module owns user/product associations; mobile owns only transient state and an account-scoped cache. No cross-module ORM imports into UI or shared-types; repositories stay behind service boundaries.

### Common rules for the seven catalog entities

- All seven persistent entities use UUID primary keys generated by PostgreSQL `uuidv7()` and UTC `timestamptz` audit fields. Do not add client UUIDv4 defaults. Mutable catalog rows use a `revision BIGINT NOT NULL` counter for publication concurrency checks. Price records are immutable; publication receipt contents are immutable except for the one-way applied-to-revoked lifecycle, guarded by a conditional status update and transaction locks. These two records do not use a mutable revision counter.
- Required text uses bounded lengths and nonblank validation. Currency is exactly `INR` in P2-A. Prices are positive BIGINT paise; no float parsing/storage/calculation, implicit currency conversion, or rounding of excessive precision. [PostgreSQL 18 numeric types](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/datatype-numeric.html) distinguish integer/exact numeric from inexact floating types.
- Foreign keys default to RESTRICT; explicitly index referencing columns used for reads. Archive/unpublish rather than cascade-delete referenced catalog or audit history. Hard deletion is a reviewed maintenance action after retention/rights review, never an API endpoint in P2-A.
- Local row invariants belong in NOT NULL/CHECK/UNIQUE constraints; cross-row visibility/authorization rules belong in transactional services plus FK/unique constraints where expressible. Do not implement cross-table CHECK expressions. [PostgreSQL constraint guidance](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/ddl-constraints.html).
- Publication may update existing rows only against expected revisions. Price and source provenance are append-only. Records removed from a source document are **not** implicitly deleted or unpublished.
- Data rights concern merchant/product content even when there is no customer PII. Never store credentials in catalog fields, audit JSON, source URLs, or uploaded material.

### Entity specifications

#### Brand (catalog)

Purpose: canonical brand identity, distinct from the seller. Fields: `id`, `slug VARCHAR(80)`, `display_name VARCHAR(160)`, `status active|archived`, `source_ref VARCHAR(200)`, `reviewer_ref VARCHAR(80)`, common timestamps/revision. Slug is normalized lowercase ASCII, unique; names are not unique and never used as deduplication IDs. Reviewer/source references identify a protected approval record, not an email or raw license document.

One brand has many products. Index unique slug and status for facets. Archive removes its products from public eligibility without deleting related rows. No ownership is inferred from a merchant name/domain matching the brand. New/merged brand identities require operator review; no automatic fuzzy brand creation. Reverse migration can drop an empty table only after dependent tables; populated environments use forward corrections, not destructive downgrade.

#### Merchant (merchant registry)

Purpose: seller/source identity for listings, not a merchant user/account or marketplace. Fields: `id`, `slug VARCHAR(80) UNIQUE`, `display_name VARCHAR(160)`, `status active|suspended`, `purchase_mode` constrained to `external_redirect` for the initial rollout (future hybrid activation requires an explicit new migration), `allowed_destination_hosts TEXT[]`, `allowed_image_hosts TEXT[]`, `source_ref`, `reviewer_ref`, timestamps/revision. Host lists must be nonempty normalized hostnames, exact-match only, without schemes, wildcards, userinfo or IP literals. Bounded maximum 20 hosts each; service validates each entry.

One merchant has many listings and publication records. Unique slug and status indexes support lookup/visibility. Brand and merchant are not one-to-one: a merchant can sell multiple approved brands, but only an operator may approve the source rights for those associations. There is no merchant self-service authorization in P2-A; approval provenance is recorded in each publication. Suspending the merchant hides every listing; existing publication history remains. No tax, bank, payment or login fields. Restore suspension by a reviewed publication; destructive rollback only in an isolated empty test database.

#### CanonicalProduct (catalog)

Purpose: one brand/style identity independent of seller, price and size. Fields: `id`, `brand_id FK Brand`, `canonical_key VARCHAR(120)`, `title VARCHAR(200)`, `description TEXT` (plain text, max 5,000 characters), `category_code VARCHAR(40)`, `status draft|published|archived`, `last_publication_id FK CatalogPublication`, timestamps/revision.

UNIQUE `(brand_id, canonical_key)`; key comes from an approved manufacturer/style identifier or an explicitly curated stable identifier, never a title hash alone. Controlled category codes are versioned in application contracts; D2 selects the initial taxonomy. CHECK constrains status; category membership is validated by the service. Index `(status, category_code, id)` and `(brand_id, status, id)`; literal title search is bounded, without pretending these B-trees accelerate arbitrary substrings.

Brand 1:N products; product 1:N listings. No FK directly to price or merchant. Publication status is not enough for discovery without an eligible listing/variant. Archive hides public details (404) but keeps identity for unavailable saved-product handling. Do not merge/delete canonical IDs automatically: stable wishlist links depend on them. Corrections to brand/style identity require manual conflict resolution before publication. Migration introduces a new table, no backfill from shell text or TS enums.

#### MerchantListing (catalog, seller association through merchant service)

Purpose: a merchant's offer of a canonical product. Fields: `id`, `product_id FK CanonicalProduct`, `merchant_id FK Merchant`, `source_listing_key VARCHAR(160)`, `destination_url TEXT` (max 2,048), `status draft|published|withdrawn`, `last_publication_id FK CatalogPublication`, timestamps/revision.

UNIQUE `(merchant_id, source_listing_key)`; multiple real listings of one product can exist, so do not impose uniqueness on product+merchant. Index `(product_id, status, merchant_id)` and `(merchant_id, status, id)`. Destination must pass the approved merchant host policy; no automatic URL fetching or redirects on the API. Listing belongs to exactly one product and merchant; remapping an existing source key to another product is a conflict requiring explicit review, not an upsert side effect.

One listing has many variants. Withdraw hides offers but retains prices and source history. Product brand/source rights must be checked at publication, not inferred from the URL. No orders or purchase guarantees. Reverse migration requires dependent variant/price tables to be empty/dropped in tests only; deployed rollback hides publication and uses forward fixes.

#### ListingVariant (catalog)

Purpose: a specific merchant option/SKU, not a global stock unit. Fields: `id`, `listing_id FK MerchantListing`, `source_variant_key VARCHAR(160)`, `size_label VARCHAR(80) NULL`, `color_label VARCHAR(80) NULL`, `color_family VARCHAR(40) NULL`, `image_url TEXT` (max 2,048), `image_alt VARCHAR(240)`, `status active|withdrawn`, `last_publication_id FK CatalogPublication`, timestamps/revision.

UNIQUE `(listing_id, source_variant_key)`; index `(listing_id, status, id)` and filter columns only after query measurement. Do not make nullable size/color a global uniqueness key. Several SKUs can share labels; conflicting duplicates within a source are operator errors. Unknown size/color stays NULL; `one_size` is allowed only when the source explicitly says so. Every published variant requires a licensed, approved image representing that variant's color; sizes may share an image. URLs use the merchant image-host policy. No quantity, synthesized stock flag, or current-price column.

Listing 1:N variants; variant 1:N price records. Withdrawn variants are excluded from filters/pricing; retained IDs support history. No client can request a variant from another product and receive it under the current product detail. Source deletion does not silently withdraw rows. Migration creates variant fields and FK/indexes; no guesses to backfill unknown sizes.

#### PriceRecord (catalog)

Purpose: immutable evidence of an approved displayed variant price. Fields: `id`, `variant_id FK ListingVariant`, `publication_id FK CatalogPublication`, `amount_minor BIGINT`, `currency CHAR(3)`, `observed_at TIMESTAMPTZ`, `valid_until TIMESTAMPTZ`, `recorded_at TIMESTAMPTZ` (database clock). UNIQUE `(publication_id, variant_id)`; CHECK amount > 0, currency = INR, valid_until > observed_at. Service rejects future observed times beyond a documented clock tolerance and non-approved freshness intervals. Index `(variant_id, recorded_at DESC, id DESC)`.

Select the latest recorded price by recorded_at/id **before** checking publication status or expiry. It is usable only while its publication is applied and its validity interval includes the request time. An expired latest record yields price NULL / stale; a revoked record, absent record or not-yet-valid record yields NULL / missing. Never skip such a record to select an older price. Proposed freshness limit is seven days from verified observation (D5). Source reports must describe consumer-facing tax-inclusive INR price; shipping/discount/tax math is not inferred. No strike-through MSRP, historical-low claims or discounts in P2-A.

Append a correction rather than changing a historical amount. A revoked publication cannot supply an active price; revocation also unpublishes its affected listings, requiring a new reviewed publication with a fresh price record for each reactivated variant before visibility resumes. Parent deletion is restricted. Data retention must support price/source review; no customer transaction data is stored. Reverse schema migration would destroy evidence, so never run it on populated user databases.

#### CatalogPublication (catalog operations, not a general ingestion job)

Purpose: small durable record of an explicitly approved operator action and retry identity. Fields: `id`, `merchant_id FK Merchant`, `request_key UUID UNIQUE` (caller-stable idempotency key), `manifest_sha256 CHAR(64)`, `schema_version SMALLINT`, `reviewer_ref VARCHAR(80)`, `approval_ref VARCHAR(200)`, `status applied|revoked`, `applied_at TIMESTAMPTZ`, `revoked_at TIMESTAMPTZ NULL`, `revocation_audit JSONB NULL` (operator ref, reason code, protected approval ref only), bounded `change_summary JSONB` (entity IDs, prior/next revisions, changed field names, counts; no arbitrary input dumps). `(merchant_id, applied_at DESC)` index; status/time/revocation-audit consistency CHECKs. Optional `supersedes_id FK CatalogPublication` documents a correction and must refer to the same merchant through service validation.

One publication affects many catalog rows and prices, but only one merchant's listings. The same request_key+digest returns the existing receipt; same key with a different digest is a conflict. Digest alone is not unique because a later intentional publication may legitimately repeat content with fresh approval. Master brand/merchant creation is separately reviewed in the same transaction before the publication receipt is inserted; do not let arbitrary source rows create those identities.

The admin UI and optional CLI use one service that validates and previews without database mutations, then records only successfully applied transactions. Failed previews/attempts produce redacted operator reports retained in access-controlled operational storage; they are not success rows. The approved canonical manifest and approval record are retained outside Git, referenced by digest/approval_ref. They are not customer-accessible. No file uploads, raw workbook blobs, queues, workers or per-row job tables. Administrative endpoints are authenticated and privileged, never available to anonymous or ordinary customer sessions.

Archive/revocation never deletes the audit receipt. Revoke conservatively unpublishes affected listings, checks that revisions have not moved, and requires a reviewed correction when later publications exist; it never replays a stale before-image over newer edits. This is content rollback, not Alembic downgrade. Proposed source/approval retention: 90 days after withdrawal, subject to rights/privacy review (D5).

Canonical products and brands can be shared across merchants. A correction to shared content must preview all affected listings, lock/check shared row revisions, and never restore a master row from one merchant's stale receipt. For an urgent shared-content rights/error incident, explicitly archive the affected product/brand across merchants pending review; listing-only revocation is insufficient. Test this separately from single-merchant price revocation. Record a revocation's operator, reason code and protected approval reference in a bounded lifecycle audit entry on the receipt, without changing its original apply evidence.

### Why these seven catalog tables, and when

### Local versus authenticated wishlist behavior

Approved P2-A (D9, 2026-10-15): only a verified signed-in account can read or mutate its remote wishlist. Right-save, remove and explicit clear wait for server acknowledgement. Independent devices signed into the same account see changes on foreground/open/refresh; no realtime push is promised. Two devices' conflicting operations resolve in database commit order; clients do not replay offline mutations or old caches over server state. No queued offline writes in this milestone.

Local cache is optional convenience: version, account namespace, product IDs, saved_at and last successful refresh only; maximum 200. It is cleared on logout/account switch/deletion. Offline display explicitly says cached/unavailable-to-refresh, never displays stale prices as current, and disables save/remove/clear until online. Local cache loss does not delete server saves. AsyncStorage may hold these non-secret fields, never contact identifiers or session tokens; it is unencrypted ([Expo AsyncStorage](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/sdk/async-storage/)). Secure session storage is specified separately. Guests have no wishlist persistence, authoritative local saves, silent merge or account-specific guest session. Existing repository contains no guest-save data migration.

### Guest browsing and transition to an authenticated save

Guest access returns only public eligible catalog data under the same publication, merchant, rights, variant and price-freshness rules as signed-in discovery. Guests may use left/similar, up/different-brand, tap/detail, search/filter and approved external retailer links without registering. Right/save preserves the current card and opens sign-in; it is not a successful save or an advance to the next card.

Keep only the current public product/card identifier and pending action in transient client UI state across authentication. This is neither a guest wishlist nor an account-specific guest session, durable save queue or server account. OAuth transaction state remains security state for sign-in, not a guest account. Cancellation or authentication failure returns to the preserved card without a write; restart may discard the transient intent without losing any saved data. After successful authentication, show the product and intended signed-in account and require explicit save confirmation. Revalidate eligibility and account authorization on the backend, apply the idempotent save, then report success/advance only after acknowledgement. A withdrawn product, expired session, network error or declined confirmation never silently saves, substitutes or advances; keep a safe retry/unavailable state. Do not expose withdrawn details beyond public visibility rules or merge a prior account's cache.

Guests cannot read or mutate wishlists, account information, grants, audit records, admin operations or unpublished/private content. Account-specific routes reject missing/invalid/expired sessions with 401; authenticated users without admin permission receive 403. Public catalog resolution by product IDs does not read a wishlist or disclose who saved a product. Public browsing must remain usable without Cognito signup/login availability when catalog dependencies and abuse protection are healthy; an authentication outage never weakens private access. Public cursor and ephemeral rate-limit state carry no account-specific guest identity.

## 5. Catalog population and deferred ingestion

**Spreadsheet ingestion is deferred.** P2-A does not accept XLSX/CSV uploads, formulas, macros, archives or attachments; there are no spreadsheet columns or upload routes to implement. A future ingestion specification must cover ownership, row staging, formula/file threats, partial-success policy, preview/approval, retry and revocation before openpyxl is used. Installed openpyxl confers no authorization to start that work.

Initial catalog plan: owner obtains a small licensed/permissioned set of real product metadata, consumer prices, variant identifiers, images and merchant destinations. A named admin prepares bounded catalog forms under an approved source agreement; an operator may also prepare a UTF-8 JSON manifest for bootstrap/operations using exactly the same validated entities. Real manifests/images are not checked into source control. Recommend at least 12 real products from at least two approved brands and multiple same-category alternatives for a useful pilot; if supply is smaller, do not invent records to reach a metric. Supply/readiness is an owner dependency (D2/D3).

### Required admin workflow and optional CLI adapter

The initial web portal supports authorized catalog list/search, inspection of provenance/freshness, bounded manual create/edit forms for brands, merchants, products, listings, variants and price observations, preview of affected visibility, explicit publish confirmation, and withdrawal/revocation. These forms operate on one shared PostgreSQL catalog; there is no customer-specific dataset or frontend product seed. Existing records are loaded with revisions. Unsaved form state is browser memory only in the first version; show a leave-page warning. No durable collaborative drafts or uploads are required. Explicitly applying a draft/unpublished change still creates an audit receipt but never makes it discoverable.

Authentication alone does not grant admin access. Every admin endpoint checks the named account's current AdminGrant and sufficiently recent strong-auth assurance; ordinary customer sessions receive 403. Actor/approval references are server-derived or verified, never trusted from editable hidden form fields. The admin browser holds no database credentials or deployment/environment selector. Requests are bound to the deployment target; publisher operations use a restricted server role. Grant provisioning/revocation stays in an audited owner-controlled operational process.

The UI serializes a bounded change set into the publication service: validate -> preview errors/diff -> confirm exact digest and expected revisions -> atomic apply -> immutable receipt. All invalid fields are visible; no silent row skip. No durable save is claimed before confirmation. The optional JSON CLI invokes this service under an operator identity for initial bootstrap or incident operations; it cannot bypass authorization, rights, constraints or auditing. A working CLI alone does not meet admin UI acceptance.

The bounded publication payload is not an open ingestion platform:

1. Envelope: schema_version, request_key, merchant reference, approval_ref, reviewed source ref, expected revisions, and products/listings/variants/prices with the keys/fields specified above. The merchant reference selects an existing ID or an explicitly approved new registry block keyed by unique slug; separately approved brand registry blocks similarly support the initial empty catalog. New IDs are generated by PostgreSQL within the apply transaction and returned in the receipt; preview uses stable source keys. Arbitrary product rows never create master identities. Max 1 MiB, 100 products and 500 variants per request, nesting bounded, unknown fields rejected. These are proposed technical safety limits, not product scale promises.
2. Normalize whitespace and Unicode for display; normalize slugs/hosts separately. Keep case-sensitive source IDs per source contract. Do not collapse distinct SKUs by title, strip meaningful size punctuation, infer brand ownership or convert sizes between brands. Same brand/style key across sellers is an explicit operator-reviewed canonical match, not fuzzy auto-merge.
3. Price input is a base-10 paise digit string; an offline rupee conversion must use Decimal and reject more than two fractional digits rather than round. Accept only INR, positive values within BIGINT and the owner-approved sanity ceiling. Display times must come from source observation, not fabricated freshness. Zero, negatives, NaN, exponent notation and mixed currencies fail validation.
4. HTTPS URLs only; bounded lengths; reject userinfo, secrets/tokens in URLs, IP literals, localhost/private/link-local destinations, non-HTTPS schemes and unapproved hosts. Validate IDNA/canonical host comparisons. Use only owner-approved image/source hosts; no API image proxy or backend downloading in P2-A. Image rights and public accessibility require operator review. External redirects after leaving DRIP are a residual risk; do not claim the app can guarantee the seller's entire redirect chain. A future server fetcher requires separate SSRF/DNS/redirect controls.
5. Preview resolves FK identities, all revision conflicts, canonical/variant duplicates, missing images/prices, and affected visibility. Produce structured errors `{path, source_key, code, safe_message}` for every invalid record; no echoed secrets or formula evaluation. Reject the whole manifest on any error. There is no silent row skip, partial publish or substitution.
6. Confirmation binds to preview digest, environment/database identity, merchant, approval_ref and expected revisions. A changed file or database revision invalidates the preview. Production execution requires a named authorized operator: the admin UI confirms its server-bound environment, while the CLI requires an operator-specific deployment identity and explicit environment confirmation; default is preview/dry-run. Catalog reads use a SELECT-only role; identity/wishlist writes and privileged publication use distinct narrowly scoped capabilities; migrations use a separate role. No embedded credentials.
7. Apply in one transaction, locking affected rows and serializing competing publication operations for the same merchant. Idempotency uniqueness plus content hash makes lost-response retries safe. Failure rolls back every catalog/price/audit mutation. No external side effects within the transaction. Failure reports remain operator artifacts; only successful receipts are returned as applied.
8. Retain redacted preview, approved manifest digest and receipt. Corrections use a new approved publication; unpublish/revoke for urgent rights or bad-data incidents. No destructive rewind or broad database reset. Original audit history is retained under the agreed policy.

For development, the same publisher can target an explicitly isolated development database with authorized real sample data. Automated tests may construct clearly labeled fixtures only inside disposable test databases; fixtures must never ship as a production seed or fallback. No product fixtures are created by this planning task. Empty production tables produce an empty catalog response and incomplete launch acceptance.

## 6. API and shared-contract proposal

Base path `/api/v1`. Catalog endpoints below are read-only and available without an account to guests as well as signed-in customers (D8 approved 2026-10-15). They expose only public eligible catalog data; there is no guest identity/session requirement. Identity, own-wishlist and privileged admin endpoints are separate capabilities described below; authentication never grants catalog-write permission. Catalog query role is SELECT-only. Apply bounded request sizes and per-source rate limits (proposed 60 requests/minute, burst 10); missing rate-limit protection fails closed with 503, not unlimited reads. Redis may hold expiring counters only. Raw IPs/search queries/seen IDs are not persisted in product analytics or logs; operational edge-log retention must be approved before pilot release. Configure proxy and application access logs to omit query strings, cursors and request bodies; test this rather than relying only on application log redaction.

Every response has explicit Pydantic models, with only public fields. [FastAPI response-model documentation](https://fastapi.tiangolo.com/tutorial/response-model/) describes response validation/filtering; never return ORM objects or approval/audit fields as unconstrained dicts. Use the established `{code,message,details,timestamp}` envelope; validation details stay safe `loc/msg/type`. Standard errors: 401 missing/expired/revoked session, 403 insufficient privilege, 409 stale revision/conflict, 413 oversized body, 415 unsupported content type, 422 malformed inputs/unknown filter codes, 404 unpublished/unknown product indistinguishably, 429 with Retry-After, 503 dependency unavailable, redacted 500. Empty matches are 200, not 404 or a fallback dataset.

### Public shapes (contract notation, not implementation)

- `MoneyINR = {currency: "INR", amount_minor: decimal_digit_string}`. Parse/format with integer/string operations; no Number conversion or floating arithmetic. Plan an explicit replacement/deprecation of existing `MoneyAmount.amount_minor: number` for catalog usage and tests across Python/TS. Do not silently change the existing shared contract during planning.
- `Price = {money: MoneyINR, observed_at: ISO_UTC, valid_until: ISO_UTC}`; `price: Price | null`, with `price_state: "current" | "stale" | "missing"`. `inventory: null` is explicit in P2-A. No stock counts or claim that a size is available.
- `ProductCard = {id, brand:{id,name}, title, category_code, image:{url,alt}, representative:{listing_id,variant_id}, price:Price, price_is_from:boolean, inventory:null}`. Exactly one canonical ID per returned page. If filtered variants have no fresh price, no card is eligible.
- `ProductDetail = {id,brand,title,description,category_code,listings:[{id,merchant:{id,name},purchase_mode:"external_redirect",destination_url:null|string,variants:[{id,size_label:null|string,color_label:null|string,image,price:null|Price,price_state,inventory:null}]}]}`. Only active brands/merchants, published products/listings and active variants; max 10 listings and 100 active variants per product for the pilot, enforced before publication. A product with a visible listing/variant but no current price can still show details with unavailable prices. Otherwise return 404, including archived brands/products. Until a destination satisfies D4 approval, destination_url is NULL in public responses and the action is omitted; protected listing provenance remains.
- `DiscoveryPage = {items:ProductCard[], next_cursor:null|string, empty_reason:null|enum, catalog_as_of:ISO_UTC}`. `catalog_as_of` is response observation time, not a stock or complete catalog-snapshot guarantee.
- `SavedResolution = {items:ProductCard[], unavailable_ids:UUID[]}` resolves requested catalog IDs only; it does not confer access to anyone's wishlist. `Wishlist = {items:[{product_id,saved_at,product:ProductCard|null,availability:"visible"|"unavailable"}], refreshed_at:ISO_UTC}` is scoped to the authenticated account, including unavailable saved IDs without hidden content. Offline cached state is labeled and never presents stale prices as current.

Shared-types would add public DTOs/enums only, never ORM models, validators coupled to React, internal permissions or database access. Types are compile-time contracts, not runtime validation. Backend serialization tests and fixture-driven TS contract tests must detect UUID/decimal-string/nullability drift. The existing broader PurchaseMode and ListingStatus unions do not define the new publication lifecycle or enable other purchase modes; specify catalog-specific contracts deliberately rather than silently reusing incompatible enums.

Discovery protocol detail: P2-A returns **one card per response**, marking its ID seen. Left/up requests the chosen intent against that anchor; right/save waits for the server wishlist acknowledgement, then requests ordinary next. No speculative batch prefetch or discarded unseen cards in the initial milestone. A later performance change may add bounded batches only with a separately reviewed protocol/test update. The next cursor includes cumulative seen IDs and the emitted IDs eligible as anchors; cap total at 200 (return exhaustion rather than overfill). Filters and limit are bound to the cursor, and visibility is rechecked on every page. Replaying/forking an old cursor can return the same IDs; the client deduplicates IDs, advances one cursor chain and ignores cancelled/stale responses. This is a bounded, stateless pilot protocol, not a stable full-catalog snapshot or persistent recommendation session. UUID ordering is a deterministic tie-breaker, not product relevance or freshness. Test rapid left/up/right actions, retries, filter changes and cancellation of stale network responses; gesture state permits only one in-flight transition.

No anonymous write, spreadsheet upload, merchant integration or click-tracking redirect endpoint is proposed. For destinations approved under D4, the validated destination URL is opened directly with an explicit seller label and without requiring DRIP sign-in; the retailer may independently require authentication on its own website; it does not silently navigate on a swipe. Search/filter values are transient and omitted from logs.

### Cognito authentication/security contract and superseded proposal

The original approved proposal used phone/email OTP request/verify endpoints, an unspecified managed provider and a simple opaque-session model without provider refresh. The owner superseded the login methods/provider on 2026-10-14. Those original assumptions are historical, not current requirements. The seven-day policy remains approved; refresh, revocation and reauthentication semantics must now be reconciled with Cognito.

[ADR 0002](adr/0002-cognito-authentication-decision.md) is the controlling authentication decision: separate customer/admin pool responsibilities; verified Cognito tokens; seven-day enforcement and immediate DRIP revocation; verified-email recovery; MFA enrollment/recovery; proof-based linking/collision handling; deletion within 30 days; minimal audit and abuse protection. Passwords belong to Cognito. Phone numbers are not required or collected in initial customer authentication; email verification/recovery does not introduce normal SMS OTP login.

The approved facts are Cognito, Google plus email/password, separate admin authentication with strong MFA and explicit grants, and the owner's lifecycle/privacy requirements. The proposed defaults (managed login/code + PKCE, separate pools, TOTP-only local admins, token lifetimes, opaque-session exchange, Mumbai/SES and explicit linking) are not deployed or automatically approved. C1-C7 must be reviewed before the affected authentication implementation. No JWT claim, provider configuration, seven-day session behavior or immediate external revocation is claimed verified without the required tests.

## 7. Security and privacy requirements

- Retain production configuration rejection, log redaction, safe HTTP envelopes and disabled production API docs. Extend tests for every new failure mode; do not weaken existing handlers to expose validator input for convenience.
- Public catalog endpoints serve guests and signed-in customers only eligible published data; guest calls cannot access account information, wishlists or any private content. UUIDv7 is not authorization. Anonymous callers cannot preview drafts, publish, change prices, retrieve audit records or select another environment. Publisher credentials are injected at deployment, never stored in data files or shipped in mobile/web bundles.
- Customer input is Google federation or email/password through Cognito; no phone collection or routine SMS OTP. Minimize retained identity metadata, enforce the 30-day private-deletion target and approved minimal audit retention. Wishlist reads/writes are account-private. No advertising IDs or persisted swipe history. Account caches clear on logout/switch. Content images contact approved third-party hosts and can disclose network metadata; disclose this and obtain rights/hosting approval (D3).
